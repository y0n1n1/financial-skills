# Contrarian Check Lens

**Core Question:** What's the strongest argument AGAINST your theory?

This is the most important lens. It prevents confirmation bias — the tendency to only look for evidence that supports what you already believe.

## Process

This is NOT a data-gathering exercise like the other lenses. This is a thinking exercise backed by targeted research.

### Step 1: Steel-man the bear case
Search for: "[TICKER] bear case" and "[TICKER] risks" and "why [TICKER] could fail" and "[TICKER] short thesis"

Find the smartest, most compelling arguments against the theory. Not strawmen — the BEST counter-arguments.

### Step 2: For each bear argument, assess
- Is this a real risk or FUD?
- Does the theory account for this?
- What's the probability this bear case plays out?
- If it does play out, how bad is it? (minor setback vs theory-breaking)

### Step 3: Identify the theory's biggest vulnerability
Every theory has a single point of failure — the one thing that, if it happened, would make the entire thesis wrong. Find it and name it explicitly.

## Common Bear Cases to Check

- **Valuation bear**: "it's a great company but way overpriced at current levels"
- **Competition bear**: "competitor X is about to eat their lunch"
- **Macro bear**: "the entire sector is in a bubble that's about to pop"
- **Execution bear**: "management is making bad decisions"
- **Disruption bear**: "new technology/approach makes their product obsolete"
- **Regulatory bear**: "government is about to crack down"
- **Customer concentration bear**: "too dependent on a few big customers"

## How to Present

Don't soften the bear case. Present it at full strength. Gabriel needs to see the real risk, not a watered-down version that makes him feel better about his position.

Then — and only then — assess whether the bear case is strong enough to undermine the theory.

## Output Format

Structure:
```
### The Bear Case
[Present the strongest 2-3 arguments against the theory at full strength]

### The Single Point of Failure
[Name the ONE thing that would break this theory]

### Assessment
[Your honest evaluation: is the bear case compelling enough to undermine the theory?]
```

Then:
```
**Verdict:** SUPPORTS/NEUTRAL/UNDERMINES — [one sentence]
```

- SUPPORTS = bear case is weak, theory survives scrutiny
- NEUTRAL = bear case has merit but doesn't break the theory
- UNDERMINES = bear case is strong enough that the theory might be wrong

Example: "**Verdict:** SUPPORTS — The strongest bear case (valuation) doesn't hold when you account for revenue growth rate. The competition bear (AMD MI300) is real but 2+ years from threatening CUDA lock-in. Single point of failure: if hyperscaler capex suddenly drops 30%+, but no evidence of this."
