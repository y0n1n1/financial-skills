# Competitive Moat Lens

**Core Question:** How defensible is the position the theory claims?

## What to Gather (via WebSearch)

Search for: "[TICKER] competitors market share" and "[TICKER] competitive advantage moat" and "[COMPANY] vs [TOP COMPETITOR]"

Pull:
- **Market share**: what % of the relevant market does this company own?
- **Competitors**: who are the top 3 threats and what are they doing?
- **Switching costs**: how hard is it for customers to leave?
- **Network effects**: does the product get better with more users?
- **IP/Patents**: any defensible intellectual property?
- **Scale advantages**: does size create cost advantages competitors can't match?
- **Ecosystem lock-in**: are customers embedded in a broader ecosystem?

## How to Analyze

The theory makes a claim about the company's position. Stress-test it:
- If theory says "monopoly" → what's the actual market share? Is it growing or shrinking? Who's the #2 and how far behind?
- If theory says "no one can compete" → search for recent competitor announcements. Any credible threats?
- If theory says "ecosystem lock-in" → how painful would switching actually be? Has anyone switched successfully?
- If theory says "best technology" → is that advantage durable or is someone 12 months behind?

Be especially skeptical here. Every company looks like it has a moat until it doesn't. Search for the COUNTER-narrative too.

## Red Flags to Watch
- Market share declining even slightly (the moat is leaking)
- A well-funded competitor making real progress (not just press releases)
- Customers publicly evaluating alternatives
- Open-source alternatives gaining traction
- Regulatory pressure that could force interoperability (killing lock-in)

## Output Format

2-4 paragraphs analyzing the competitive position relative to the theory's claims.

Then:
```
**Verdict:** SUPPORTS/NEUTRAL/UNDERMINES — [one sentence]
```

Example: "**Verdict:** SUPPORTS — 80%+ GPU market share with CUDA ecosystem lock-in. AMD's MI300 is competitive on paper but enterprise migration costs are massive. No credible threat to the monopoly within the theory's 3-year timeline."
