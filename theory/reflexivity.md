# Reflexivity Check

**Core Question:** How would a significant price move (up or down) feed back into the very fundamentals that support your theory?

From George Soros' Theory of Reflexivity: markets are not passive mirrors of reality. Price movements can CHANGE the fundamentals they're supposed to reflect. Your theory assumes certain fundamentals — but those fundamentals are not independent of the stock price.

## Why This Matters

Classical analysis treats fundamentals and price as separate: fundamentals → price. Soros showed it's circular: fundamentals ↔ price.

Examples:
- **Positive reflexivity**: stock goes up → company raises capital cheaply → funds R&D → fundamentals improve → stock goes up more (bubble formation)
- **Negative reflexivity**: stock drops → employees leave (stock comp worthless) → talent drain → execution suffers → fundamentals deteriorate → stock drops more (doom loop)
- **Thesis-breaking reflexivity**: you bet on the company's ability to acquire competitors → stock drops → can't use stock as acquisition currency → theory breaks even if the company itself is fine

## Process

### Step 1: Map the Reflexive Links
For the specific theory, identify HOW the stock price connects back to fundamentals:

| Price Channel | How It Feeds Back |
|--------------|-------------------|
| **Talent** | Stock comp value → employee retention → execution quality |
| **Capital** | Stock price → ability to raise equity → fund growth/acquisitions |
| **Customer confidence** | Stock price → perceived stability → enterprise sales cycles |
| **Supplier terms** | Stock price → creditworthiness → supplier willingness to extend terms |
| **Competitive position** | Stock price → acquisition currency → ability to buy threats |
| **Narrative** | Stock price → media coverage → sector sentiment → multiple expansion/compression |

Not all channels apply to every stock. Identify which are RELEVANT to THIS theory.

### Step 2: Run Two Scenarios

**Scenario A: Price drops 30-40%**
For each relevant reflexive channel:
- What happens to this channel if the stock drops 30-40%?
- Does this damage the fundamentals that the theory depends on?
- Could this create a doom loop (negative reflexivity)?
- Rate the feedback: NONE / MILD / MODERATE / SEVERE

**Scenario B: Price rises 50%+**
For each relevant reflexive channel:
- Does success change the thesis? (e.g., at a higher price, is the risk/reward still attractive?)
- Could this create a bubble dynamic (positive reflexivity)?
- Does the company behave differently when its stock is flying? (complacency, over-expansion)
- Rate the feedback: NONE / MILD / MODERATE / SEVERE

### Step 3: Assess Reflexivity Risk

```
Overall Reflexivity Risk: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH

LOW = fundamentals are largely independent of stock price (e.g., stable cash-generating business)
MEDIUM = some channels are active but manageable (e.g., talent retention depends partly on stock comp)
HIGH = the theory critically depends on conditions that stock price itself influences (e.g., the company needs to raise capital, or uses stock for acquisitions)
```

### Step 4: Theory Implications
- Does reflexivity STRENGTHEN the theory at current prices? (positive feedback loop in play)
- Does reflexivity make the theory FRAGILE? (a price drop could break the very conditions the theory assumes)
- Should this affect position sizing? (high reflexivity risk → smaller position)

```
- [channel 2]: [how it feeds back]

[analysis of feedback effects]

If price rises 50%+:
[analysis of feedback effects]

```

## Critical Rule

Companies that NEED a high stock price to execute their strategy (growth via acquisition, attracting talent with equity, raising capital) have HIGH reflexivity risk. A price decline can become self-reinforcing. Factor this into conviction and sizing.
